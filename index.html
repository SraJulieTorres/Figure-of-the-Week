    Your analysis must: Prove the quadrilateral is a square using both side lengths and slopes, and show how the diagonals are related.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    It appears to be a square. Predicted properties: four congruent sides; four right angles; opposite sides parallel; diagonals AC and BD congruent, perpendicular, and bisecting each other at M; diagonals bisect the 90° vertex angles into 45° angles. These are informal claims from looking. The rest of the week turns them into formal ones.

    Measure it

    AB = √((4−1)² + (1−2)²) = √10
    BC = √((5−4)² + (4−1)²) = √10
    CD = √((2−5)² + (5−4)²) = √10
    DA = √((1−2)² + (2−5)²) = √10
    Diagonals: AC = √(16 + 4) = √20 = 2√5 and BD = √(4 + 16) = 2√5. The diagonals are congruent.

    Justify it

    Slopes: AB = −1/3, BC = 3, CD = −1/3, DA = 3. Each pair of consecutive sides has slopes whose product is (−1/3)(3) = −1, so consecutive sides are perpendicular and every angle is a right angle. A quadrilateral with four congruent sides and four right angles is a square.

    What if?

    Midpoint of AC: ((1+5)/2, (2+4)/2) = (3, 3). Slope of AC = 2/4 = 1/2, so the perpendicular slope is −2. Equation: y = −2x + 9.
    Check B(4, 1): −8 + 9 = 1 ✓. Check D(2, 5): −4 + 9 = 5 ✓. The perpendicular bisector of AC is line BD. Every point on a perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the endpoints, and B and D are each √10 from A and from C. In a square, each diagonal is the perpendicular bisector of the other.
  2. B Option B · Find the Error

    Jordan says, “ABCD is a rhombus but not a square, because a square’s sides have to be horizontal and vertical.”

    Explain what went wrong, correct the work, and use the figure to justify the correct answer.
    Exemplar
    Error: Jordan assumes a square must be aligned with the grid. Orientation does not change a figure’s properties.
    Correction: All four sides are √10. The slopes are −1/3, 3, −1/3, 3, and consecutive slopes multiply to −1, so every angle is 90°.
    Conclusion: Four congruent sides and four right angles make ABCD a square; a rotated square is still a square.

    Your analysis must: Decide exactly where PQRS belongs among the quadrilaterals, and analyze the figure formed by joining the midpoints of its sides.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    First guess: a rhombus (it looks like a diamond with equal sides). To be sure I need side lengths (Distance Formula) to test for congruent sides, and slopes to test for parallel and perpendicular sides. A rhombus sits in the overlap of parallelograms and kites; a square sits inside both rhombus and rectangle.

    Measure it

    PQ = √(4² + 3²) = 5, QR = √(4² + (−3)²) = 5, RS = √((−4)² + (−3)²) = 5, SP = √((−4)² + 3²) = 5.
    Slopes: PQ = 3/4, QR = −3/4, RS = 3/4, SP = −3/4.

    Justify it

    No. Opposite sides have equal slopes, so they are parallel, and all four sides are 5, so PQRS is a rhombus. Consecutive slopes multiply to (3/4)(−3/4) = −9/16, not −1, so there are no right angles and it is not a square.
    Diagonals: PR lies on y = 1 (horizontal, length 8) and QS lies on x = 1 (vertical, length 6). Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular, and both diagonals have midpoint (1, 1), so they are perpendicular bisectors of each other, as expected for a rhombus.

    What if?

    Midpoints: PQ (−1, 2.5), QR (3, 2.5), RS (3, −0.5), SP (−1, −0.5). The figure has horizontal sides of length 4 and vertical sides of length 3, so it is a rectangle (not a square).
    Area of rectangle = 4 × 3 = 12. Area of rhombus = (8 × 6)/2 = 24. The midpoint quadrilateral has exactly half the area, and its sides are parallel to the diagonals of PQRS.
  2. B Option B · Find the Error

    Maya says, “PQRS is a square because all four sides measure 5.”

    Explain what went wrong, correct the work, and use the figure to justify the correct answer.
    Exemplar
    Error: Four congruent sides only guarantee a rhombus. A square also needs right angles.
    Correction: The slopes of consecutive sides are 3/4 and −3/4. Their product is −9/16, not −1, so the angles are not right angles. The diagonals are 8 and 6, so they are not congruent either.
    Conclusion: PQRS is a rhombus, not a square.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    AB = √(36 + 4) = √40, AC = √(4 + 36) = √40, BC = √(16 + 16) = √32.
    Two congruent sides → isosceles. Longest side squared: 40 < 40 + 32, so the largest angle is less than 90° → acute. Triangle ABC is an acute isosceles triangle.

    Measure it

    P = √40 + √40 + √32 = 2√40 + √32 = 4√10 + 4√2 ≈ 18.31 units.

    Justify it

    Line AB: slope 2/6 = 1/3 → y = (1/3)x + 2/3.
    Perpendicular through C(3, 7): slope −3 → y = −3x + 16.
    Solve: (1/3)x + 2/3 = −3x + 16 → x + 2 = −9x + 48 → x = 4.6, y = 2.2. So H(4.6, 2.2).
    CH = √(1.6² + 4.8²) = √25.6 ≈ 5.06.
    Area = ½ · √40 · √25.6 = ½ · √1024 = ½ · 32 = 16 square units.

    What if?

    Box from (1, 1) to (7, 7): area 36. Subtract the three corner triangles: ½(6)(2) = 6, ½(4)(4) = 8, ½(2)(6) = 6. 36 − 20 = 16 ✓ (the area is the same no matter which side is the base).
    Dilation by k = 3: perimeter × 3 ≈ 54.93; area × 3² = 144 square units.

    Fully analyze this week’s figure in one write-up. Name and classify the figure and its parts, calculate every measurement you can, justify at least one relationship with a theorem or a proof, and end with a “what if” question that you answer yourself.

    Your analysis must: Identify both transformations, write them in function notation, and prove all three triangles are congruent.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    △J′K′L′ (blue) is a translation of △JKL 5 units right and 2 units up. △J″K″L″ (purple) is a reflection of △JKL across line ℓ: x = −1. Both are rigid motions, so both are isometries: they preserve size and shape. The reflection reverses orientation (J→K→L runs the opposite way); the translation does not.

    Measure it

    Translation: T⟨5, 2⟩(J) = J′(6, 3), T(K) = K′(9, 3), T(L) = L′(6, 5).
    Reflection: Rℓ(J) = J″(−3, 1), Rℓ(K) = K″(−6, 1), Rℓ(L) = L″(−3, 3).
    Side lengths in all three triangles: JK = 3, JL = 2, KL = √(9 + 4) = √13. Corresponding sides match, so all three triangles are congruent.

    Justify it

    L(1, 3) and L″(−3, 3). Midpoint = (−1, 3), which lies on x = −1. Segment LL″ is horizontal and ℓ is vertical, so they are perpendicular. A line that is perpendicular to a segment and passes through its midpoint is its perpendicular bisector. This is the defining property of a reflection: the line of reflection is the perpendicular bisector of every segment joining a point to its image.

    What if?

    One sequence: translate by ⟨−5, −2⟩ (back to JKL), then reflect across x = −1.
    Another: reflect across x = −1 first, giving (−8, 3), (−11, 3), (−8, 5), then translate by ⟨5, −2⟩, giving (−3, 1), (−6, 1), (−3, 3) ✓. There are many correct sequences; what matters is that some sequence exists, which proves the triangles are congruent.
  2. B Option B · Find the Error

    Ava says, “△J″K″L″ is a translation of △JKL 8 units to the left.”

    Explain what went wrong, correct the work, and use the figure to justify the correct answer.
    Exemplar
    Error: In a translation every point moves the same distance in the same direction. Here J moves 4 units left (1 → −3) but K moves 10 units left (4 → −6).
    Correction: The orientation of the triangle is reversed, and each point is the same distance from the line x = −1 as its image. △J″K″L″ is a reflection across x = −1, written Rℓ(J) = J″.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    A rotation. The image has the same size, shape, and orientation (not reversed, so not a reflection), and it is not simply shifted (so not a translation). A and A′ are both 2 units from E, which suggests E is the center of rotation.

    Measure it

    EC = √((4−2)² + (4−1)²) = √13 and EC′ = √((−1−2)² + (3−1)²) = √13.
    Slope of EC = 3/2; slope of EC′ = 2/(−3) = −2/3. The product is −1, so ∠CEC′ = 90°. The rotation is 90° counterclockwise about E.

    Justify it

    Perpendicular bisector of AA′: midpoint (3, 2), slope of AA′ = −1, so y = x − 1.
    Perpendicular bisector of CC′: midpoint (1.5, 3.5), slope of CC′ = 1/5, so y = −5x + 11.
    Solve: x − 1 = −5x + 11 → x = 2, y = 1. The center is (2, 1) = E ✓.
    AA′ and BB′ have the same perpendicular bisector (y = x − 1) because A and B lie on the same ray from E, so those two lines don’t intersect at a single point.

    What if?

    Shift so E is the origin, rotate (x, y) → (−y, x), shift back: (x, y) → (−(y − 1) + 2, (x − 2) + 1) = (3 − y, x − 1).
    A(4, 1) → (2, 3) ✓; B(6, 1) → (2, 5) ✓; C(4, 4) → (−1, 3) ✓. The origin (0, 0) → (3, −1).

    Explain what went wrong, correct the work, and use the figure to justify the correct answer.
    Exemplar

    Check: (3, 2) is √5 from C(4, 4) but √17 from C′(−1, 3), so it cannot be the center.
    Correction: Intersect two perpendicular bisectors, y = x − 1 (from AA′) and y = −5x + 11 (from CC′), to get the center E(2, 1). The rotation is 90° counterclockwise.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    TR runs from (1, 1) to (5, 1) and PA from (2, 3) to (4, 3). Both are horizontal, so TR ∥ PA. The legs: TP = √(1 + 4) = √5 and RA = √(1 + 4) = √5. Exactly one pair of parallel sides with congruent legs → isosceles trapezoid.

    Measure it

    T(1, 1) → T′(−1, 1), R(5, 1) → R′(−1, 5), A(4, 3) → A′(−3, 4), P(2, 3) → P′(−3, 2). Every point follows (x, y) → (−y, x): a 90° counterclockwise rotation about the origin.

    Justify it

    Across y-axis (x, y) → (−x, y): (−1, 1), (−5, 1), (−4, 3), (−2, 3). Then across x-axis (x, y) → (x, −y): (−1, −1), (−5, −1), (−4, −3), (−2, −3).
    180° rotation (x, y) → (−x, −y) applied to TRAP gives the same four points ✓. Two reflections across perpendicular lines through the origin compose to a 180° rotation.

    What if?

    Translate: T(−2, 3), R(2, 3), A(1, 5), P(−1, 5). Then reflect: T(−2, −3), R(2, −3), A(1, −5), P(−1, −5).

    Your analysis must: Describe every symmetry of the octagon and connect its rotation angle to its interior angle.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    Regular octagon ABCDEFGH (8 congruent sides, 8 congruent angles). It has reflectional symmetry across lines through opposite vertices (pink) and across lines through midpoints of opposite sides (blue). It also has rotational symmetry about its center O.

    Measure it

    8 lines of symmetry: 4 vertex-to-vertex and 4 midpoint-to-midpoint.
    Smallest rotation: 360°/8 = 45° (also 90°, 135°, 180°, 225°, 270°, 315°).
    Interior angle: 180(8 − 2)/8 = 135°. Note 135° + 45° = 180°.

    Justify it

    Hypothesis p: a polygon is a regular octagon. Conclusion q: it has 45° rotational symmetry. The statement is true.
    Converse: “If a polygon has 45° rotational symmetry, then it is a regular octagon.” False. Counterexample: an eight-pointed star polygon (a 16-sided figure) also maps onto itself under a 45° rotation. One counterexample is enough to show a statement is false.

    What if?

    Interior angle = 180(n − 2)/n. Smallest rotation = 360/n.
    Sum: [180(n − 2) + 360]/n = (180n − 360 + 360)/n = 180n/n = 180°. So the two angles are always supplementary. Check with n = 8: 135° + 45° = 180° ✓.
  2. B Option B · Find the Error

    Kai says, “A regular octagon has 4 lines of symmetry, one through each pair of opposite vertices.”

    Explain what went wrong, correct the work, and use the figure to justify the correct answer.
    Exemplar
    Error: Kai counted only the lines through vertices. Lines through the midpoints of opposite sides are also lines of symmetry.
    Correction: There are 4 vertex-to-vertex lines and 4 midpoint-to-midpoint lines, so 8 lines of symmetry. A regular n-gon always has n lines of symmetry. Its rotational symmetry is 360°/8 = 45°.

    Exemplar

    Name it

    Kite ABCD: two pairs of consecutive congruent sides. Given: AB ≅ AD (one tick) and CB ≅ CD (two ticks). The triangles share side AC, so AC ≅ AC by the Reflexive Property.

    Measure it

    m∠B = 180 − 37 − 56 = 87°. Because △ABC ≅ △ADC (next task), m∠DAC = 37°, m∠DCA = 56°, m∠D = 87°. m∠BAD = 37 + 37 = 74°. Check: 74 + 87 + 112 + 87 = 360 ✓ (m∠BCD = 112°).

    Justify it

    Statement Reason
    1. AB ≅ AD, CB ≅ CD Given
    2. AC ≅ AC Reflexive Property
    3. △ABC ≅ △ADC SSS Congruence Theorem
    4. ∠BAC ≅ ∠DAC CPCTC
    5. AC bisects ∠BAD Definition of angle bisector

    What if?

    No. That combination is SSA (two sides and a non-included angle), which is not a congruence theorem: two different triangles can share those three measurements. The triangles here happen to be congruent, but SSA cannot be the reason.
    The rigid motion is a reflection across line AC, which is why AC is the kite’s line of symmetry.

    Exemplar

    6 · 6 = 36 equally likely outcomes. Event A (sum of 8): (2, 6), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 3), (6, 2). Event B (first cube even): rows 2, 4, and 6. The darkest cells are A and B.

    Justify it

    First odd 2 16 18

    Measure it

  3. J Justify

    Is PQRS a square? Use slopes of the sides and the diagonals to justify your answer.

    Exemplar
    No. Opposite sides have equal slopes, so they are parallel, and all four sides are 5, so PQRS is a rhombus. Consecutive slopes multiply to (3/4)(−3/4) = −9/16, not −1, so there are no right angles and it is not a square.
    Diagonals: PR lies on y = 1 (horizontal, length 8) and QS lies on x = 1 (vertical, length 6). Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular, and both diagonals have midpoint (1, 1), so they are perpendicular bisectors of each other, as expected for a rhombus.

    Exemplar
